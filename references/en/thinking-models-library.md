# Thinking Models Library (English)

94 curated thinking models organized by 9 strategic scenarios. Each model includes core principles, applicable scenarios, and coaching prompts for GROW sessions.

## How to Use This Library

1. During Phase 0, identify the user's scenario from signal words
2. Select 2-3 models from the matched scenario
3. Embed the models naturally into GROW phases (R/O/W)
4. Use the coaching prompts to guide the user through each model

---

## S01: Cognitive Calibration (12 Models)

### 1. Confirmation Bias
- **Core Principle**: We tend to seek information that confirms our existing beliefs and ignore contradictory evidence.
- **Applicable**: Decision-making, problem analysis, data interpretation
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Let's play devil's advocate. What evidence would prove your current conclusion WRONG?"

### 2. Anchoring Effect
- **Core Principle**: The first piece of information we receive disproportionately influences our subsequent judgments.
- **Applicable**: Negotiations, pricing, estimations
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What number/idea are you anchored to? What would your answer be if you started from zero?"

### 3. Availability Heuristic
- **Core Principle**: We judge probability by how easily examples come to mind, not by actual frequency.
- **Applicable**: Risk assessment, decision-making
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Are you over-weighting recent or memorable events? What does the base data actually show?"

### 4. Hindsight Bias
- **Core Principle**: After an event occurs, we believe we "knew it all along," making past outcomes seem more predictable than they were.
- **Applicable**: Post-mortems, performance reviews
- **Coaching Prompt**: "If you didn't know the outcome, what would you have predicted? What information was actually available at the time?"

### 5. Dunning-Kruger Effect
- **Core Principle**: People with limited knowledge in an area overestimate their competence, while experts underestimate theirs.
- **Applicable**: Self-assessment, hiring, skill development
- **Coaching Prompt**: "On a scale of 1-10, how would you rate your expertise? What would someone with true expertise say differently?"

### 6. Sunk Cost Fallacy
- **Core Principle**: We continue investing in something because of already-invested resources, rather than future value.
- **Applicable**: Project decisions, career choices, investments
- **Coaching Prompt**: "If you were starting fresh today with no prior investment, would you still make this choice?"

### 7. Framing Effect
- **Core Principle**: The same information presented differently (gain vs. loss frame) leads to different decisions.
- **Applicable**: Communication, persuasion, reporting
- **Coaching Prompt**: "How are you currently framing this? What if you presented it as an opportunity instead of a problem?"

### 8. Survivor Bias
- **Core Principle**: We focus on successful examples and ignore failures, leading to distorted conclusions about what works.
- **Applicable**: Strategy analysis, best practices evaluation
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Who are the 'survivors' you're learning from? What about those who tried the same approach and failed?"

### 9. Base Rate Fallacy
- **Core Principle**: We ignore general statistical information (base rates) in favor of specific details.
- **Applicable**: Probability judgments, risk assessment
- **Coaching Prompt**: "In similar situations generally, what's the typical success rate? How does your specific case compare?"

### 10. Bandwagon Effect
- **Core Principle**: We tend to adopt beliefs and behaviors because many others do, rather than based on independent analysis.
- **Applicable**: Trend analysis, market decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Are you following this because the data supports it, or because everyone else is doing it?"

### 11. Authority Bias
- **Core Principle**: We attribute greater accuracy to the opinion of authority figures, regardless of content.
- **Applicable**: Decision-making, advice evaluation
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Are you agreeing because the evidence is strong, or because of who said it?"

### 12. Status Quo Bias
- **Core Principle**: We prefer things to stay the same, perceiving any change from the baseline as a loss.
- **Applicable**: Change management, innovation decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "If the current situation wasn't already in place, would you choose to set it up this way?"

## S02: Learning & Growth (10 Models)

### 13. Feynman Technique
- **Core Principle**: The best way to learn is to teach it simply. If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough.
- **Applicable**: Skill acquisition, knowledge verification
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Can you explain this concept in plain English as if teaching a 12-year-old? Where do you get stuck?"

### 14. Deliberate Practice
- **Core Principle**: Improvement comes from targeted practice at the edge of your ability, not from repetition alone.
- **Applicable**: Skill development, performance improvement
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's the specific sub-skill you're weakest at? Can you design a focused exercise for just that?"

### 15. Pareto Principle (80/20)
- **Core Principle**: 80% of results come from 20% of efforts. Focus on the vital few rather than the trivial many.
- **Applicable**: Priority setting, resource allocation, learning focus
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What 20% of this topic would give you 80% of the practical value?"

### 16. Spaced Repetition
- **Core Principle**: Information is retained longer when review sessions are spaced over increasing intervals.
- **Applicable**: Memory, knowledge retention
- **Coaching Prompt**: "When did you last review this? What review schedule would help you retain it long-term?"

### 17. Growth Mindset
- **Core Principle**: Abilities can be developed through dedication and hard work. "Not yet" instead of "can't."
- **Applicable**: Overcoming challenges, resilience
- **Coaching Prompt**: "You said you 'can't' do this. What if it's actually 'can't YET'? What would make it possible?"

### 18. Zone of Proximal Development
- **Core Principle**: Optimal learning happens in the zone between what you can do alone and what you can do with guidance.
- **Applicable**: Learning planning, skill progression
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Is this challenge too easy, too hard, or just right? Where's your learning edge?"

### 19. Mental Models Transfer
- **Core Principle**: Models learned in one domain can be applied to solve problems in entirely different domains.
- **Applicable**: Cross-domain innovation, problem solving
- **Coaching Prompt**: "You solved a similar problem in [domain X]. What principle did you use? Can it apply here?"

### 20. Active Recall
- **Core Principle**: Actively retrieving information from memory is more effective than passively re-reading it.
- **Applicable**: Study methods, knowledge verification
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Close your notes. What can you recall about this topic without looking?"

### 21. Interleaving Practice
- **Core Principle**: Mixing different topics or types of problems during practice improves long-term retention and transfer.
- **Applicable**: Study planning, skill development
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Are you practicing one thing repeatedly, or mixing different challenges? Try interleaving."

### 22. Meta-Learning
- **Core Principle**: Learning how to learn — understanding your own learning process to optimize it.
- **Applicable**: Learning strategy, self-improvement
- **Coaching Prompt**: "How do you learn best? What patterns do you notice in your most effective learning experiences?"

## S03: Problem Solving (10 Models)

### 23. Root Cause Analysis (5 Whys)
- **Core Principle**: Ask "why" five times in succession to move beyond symptoms to the fundamental cause.
- **Applicable**: Any problem investigation, quality issues
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Let's dig deeper. [Problem] happened. Why? [Answer]. And why is that? Keep going until we hit the root."

### 24. First Principles Thinking
- **Core Principle**: Break a problem down to its most fundamental truths and reason up from there, rather than reasoning by analogy.
- **Applicable**: Innovation, strategy, complex decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What do we know for CERTAIN is true? Strip away all assumptions. What remains?"

### 25. Inversion Thinking
- **Core Principle**: Instead of thinking about how to succeed, think about how to guarantee failure — then avoid those things.
- **Applicable**: Risk management, planning
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What would guarantee this project fails completely? Now, how do we prevent each of those things?"

### 26. Lateral Thinking
- **Core Principle**: Solve problems through indirect and creative approaches, breaking out of established patterns.
- **Applicable**: Innovation, brainstorming, creative blocks
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What would someone in a completely different industry do? What's the most unconventional approach?"

### 27. SCAMPER
- **Core Principle**: Systematic creativity technique: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse.
- **Applicable**: Product development, process improvement
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Can we Substitute any component? Combine with something else? Adapt from another domain? Eliminate something unnecessary?"

### 28. Design Thinking
- **Core Principle**: Human-centered approach: Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test.
- **Applicable**: Product design, service design, user experience
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Who is the end user? What do they actually need (not what they say they need)?"

### 29. Hypothesis-Driven Approach
- **Core Principle**: Form a clear hypothesis first, then gather data to test it. Avoid boiling the ocean.
- **Applicable**: Research, experimentation, strategy
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's your hypothesis? What data would prove it wrong? How can you test it quickly?"

### 30. MECE Principle
- **Core Principle**: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive — framework for ensuring complete analysis without overlap.
- **Applicable**: Problem decomposition, analysis frameworks
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Have you covered all possibilities? Are any of your categories overlapping?"

### 31. Occam's Razor
- **Core Principle**: Among competing explanations, the simplest one with the fewest assumptions is usually correct.
- **Applicable**: Diagnosis, troubleshooting, theory selection
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Of all possible explanations, which one requires the fewest assumptions?"

### 32. Red Teaming
- **Core Principle**: Appoint a team to challenge plans and assumptions from an adversary's perspective.
- **Applicable**: Strategy validation, plan review
- **Coaching Prompt**: "If your smartest competitor saw this plan, how would they exploit its weaknesses?"

## S04: Systems Insight (10 Models)

### 33. Flywheel Effect
- **Core Principle**: Small consistent actions compound over time, creating momentum that makes each subsequent action easier and more impactful.
- **Applicable**: Business strategy, habit formation, organizational change
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's the one action that, if repeated consistently, would make everything else easier?"

### 34. Systems Thinking
- **Core Principle**: Understand how parts of a system interact. Focus on relationships and flows, not just individual components.
- **Applicable**: Organizational analysis, complex problems
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Draw the system. What are the parts? How do they connect? Where are the feedback loops?"

### 35. Feedback Loops
- **Core Principle**: Reinforcing loops amplify change (virtuous/vicious cycles); Balancing loops stabilize the system.
- **Applicable**: Process analysis, behavior patterns
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Is this a reinforcing loop (getting stronger) or a balancing loop (self-correcting)? How can we break a vicious cycle?"

### 36. Tragedy of the Commons
- **Core Principle**: Individuals acting in their own self-interest deplete a shared resource, even when it's against everyone's long-term interest.
- **Applicable**: Resource management, team dynamics, environmental issues
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What shared resource is being depleted? What incentives are driving individual behavior?"

### 37. Network Effects
- **Core Principle**: A product or service becomes more valuable as more people use it.
- **Applicable**: Platform strategy, product design, market analysis
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Does your product get better with more users? What's the tipping point for the network effect to kick in?"

### 38. Second-Order Thinking
- **Core Principle**: Consider not just the immediate result of an action, but its subsequent consequences and cascading effects.
- **Applicable**: Decision-making, policy design, strategy
- **Coaching Prompt**: "And then what? If we do X, what happens next? And after that?"

### 39. Complexity Theory
- **Core Principle**: Complex systems exhibit emergent behaviors that cannot be predicted from understanding individual components alone.
- **Applicable**: Organizational change, market dynamics
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What emergent behaviors are you seeing that no single part of the system was designed to produce?"

### 40. Emergence
- **Core Principle**: Higher-level patterns arise from lower-level interactions. The whole is greater than the sum of its parts.
- **Applicable**: Team dynamics, innovation, culture
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What patterns are emerging from your team that no individual is responsible for?"

### 41. Leverage Points
- **Core Principle**: In any system, there are specific points where a small shift can produce large, systemic changes.
- **Applicable**: Change management, strategy, problem solving
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Where's the leverage point? What small change would produce the biggest systemic impact?"

### 42. Bottleneck Analysis
- **Core Principle**: The throughput of any system is limited by its slowest component (Theory of Constraints).
- **Applicable**: Process optimization, productivity
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's the slowest step in your process? If you fixed just that one thing, how much would overall throughput improve?"

## S05: Data & Evidence (10 Models)

### 43. Correlation ≠ Causation
- **Core Principle**: Just because two things occur together doesn't mean one causes the other. There may be a confounding variable.
- **Applicable**: Data analysis, research interpretation
- **Coaching Prompt**: "A and B happen together. But what if C causes both? What's the actual causal mechanism?"

### 44. Base Rate Fallacy
- **Core Principle**: We tend to ignore general statistical information (base rates) when presented with specific details.
- **Applicable**: Probability, risk assessment, medical decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Before considering the specific details, what's the base rate? How common is this in general?"

### 45. Simpson's Paradox
- **Core Principle**: A trend appears in different groups of data but disappears or reverses when these groups are combined.
- **Applicable**: Data analysis, A/B testing, statistics
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Have you checked whether the trend holds across all subgroups? Could combining data be hiding a reversal?"

### 46. Sample Size Bias
- **Core Principle**: Small samples produce unreliable results. Statistical significance requires adequate sample sizes.
- **Applicable**: Research evaluation, data-driven decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "How large is your sample? Is this result statistically significant or just noise?"

### 47. Regression to the Mean
- **Core Principle**: Extreme outcomes tend to be followed by more average ones, regardless of any intervention.
- **Applicable**: Performance evaluation, policy assessment
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Was the previous result an extreme? Could the current change just be regression to the mean?"

### 48. Cherry Picking
- **Core Principle**: Selectively presenting only data points that support a desired conclusion while ignoring contradictory evidence.
- **Applicable**: Critical thinking, argument evaluation
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What data are you NOT showing? What would the full picture look like?"

### 49. Texas Sharpshooter
- **Core Principle**: Firing at a barn door and drawing a bullseye around the cluster — finding patterns in random data after the fact.
- **Applicable**: Strategy analysis, pattern recognition
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Was this pattern predicted in advance, or are you finding it in hindsight?"

### 50. Bayes' Theorem
- **Core Principle**: Update your probability estimates as new evidence comes in. Prior probability × New evidence = Updated probability.
- **Applicable**: Decision-making under uncertainty, medical diagnosis
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What did you believe before this new evidence? How much should this evidence change your mind?"

### 51. Data Visualization Literacy
- **Core Principle**: The way data is visually presented can dramatically change interpretation. Watch for misleading scales, cherry-picked time ranges, and truncated axes.
- **Applicable**: Presentation review, report analysis
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Look at the axes. Does the scale start at zero? Is the time range selected to show a specific trend?"

### 52. Statistical Significance
- **Core Principle**: A result is statistically significant if it's unlikely to have occurred by chance alone (typically p < 0.05).
- **Applicable**: Research evaluation, A/B testing
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Is this difference statistically significant or could it be due to random variation?"

## S06: Decision Analysis (12 Models)

### 53. Decision Matrix
- **Core Principle**: Create a weighted matrix to evaluate multiple options against defined criteria, reducing emotional bias.
- **Applicable**: Multi-option decisions, vendor selection, hiring
- **Coaching Prompt**: "List your options as rows and evaluation criteria as columns. Weight each criterion. Score each option. What does the math say?"

### 54. Opportunity Cost
- **Core Principle**: The true cost of any choice is the value of the next best alternative you give up.
- **Applicable**: Resource allocation, time management, investment
- **Coaching Prompt**: "By choosing this, what are you giving up? What's your next best alternative?"

### 55. Expected Value
- **Core Principle**: Multiply the probability of each outcome by its value. Choose the option with the highest expected value.
- **Applicable**: Risk-reward analysis, investment decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What are the possible outcomes? What's the probability and value of each? What's the expected value?"

### 56. Minimax Principle
- **Core Principle**: Choose the option that minimizes the maximum possible loss. Useful when downside protection matters most.
- **Applicable**: Risk-averse decisions, competitive strategy
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's the worst-case scenario for each option? Which option has the least-bad worst case?"

### 57. Sunk Cost (in decisions)
- **Core Principle**: Past investments should not influence future decisions. Only future costs and benefits matter.
- **Applicable**: Project continuation, career decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Imagine you haven't made any investment yet. Would you still choose this path?"

### 58. Reversibility Test
- **Core Principle**: Distinguish between one-way doors (irreversible) and two-way doors (reversible). Decide accordingly.
- **Applicable**: Strategic decisions, organizational change
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Is this a one-way door or a two-way door? If it's reversible, what are you waiting for?"

### 59. 10/10/10 Rule
- **Core Principle**: Evaluate a decision by considering how you'll feel about it 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years from now.
- **Applicable**: Emotional decisions, life choices
- **Coaching Prompt**: "How will you feel about this decision in 10 minutes? 10 months? 10 years?"

### 60. Prospect Theory
- **Core Principle**: People feel losses approximately twice as strongly as equivalent gains. Loss aversion drives many irrational decisions.
- **Applicable**: Negotiation, behavioral economics, pricing
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Are you avoiding a loss or seeking a gain? Would you make the same decision if the amounts were reversed?"

### 61. Asymmetric Upside
- **Core Principle**: Seek decisions where the potential upside massively outweighs the potential downside.
- **Applicable**: Investment, career, innovation
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's the best case? What's the worst case? Is the upside disproportionately larger?"

### 62. Pre-mortem Analysis
- **Core Principle**: Before making a decision, imagine it has failed spectacularly. Work backwards to identify what went wrong.
- **Applicable**: Project planning, strategic decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "It's one year from now and this has been a disaster. What went wrong?"

### 63. Marginal Thinking
- **Core Principle**: Make decisions at the margin — consider the cost and benefit of one additional unit, not the total.
- **Applicable**: Pricing, resource allocation, optimization
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Not 'should we do this?' but 'should we do one MORE of this?' What's the marginal benefit vs. marginal cost?"

### 64. Opportunity Tree
- **Core Principle**: Map out the decision tree of possible choices and outcomes, assigning probabilities to each branch.
- **Applicable**: Complex multi-stage decisions
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Map out each choice as a branch. What are the possible outcomes at each branch? How likely is each?"

## S07: Conflict Resolution (10 Models)

### 65. Game Theory
- **Core Principle**: Analyze strategic interactions where the outcome for each participant depends on the choices of all.
- **Applicable**: Negotiations, competitive strategy, organizational dynamics
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What are the players? What are their possible moves? What's the Nash Equilibrium?"

### 66. Nash Equilibrium
- **Core Principle**: A state where no player can improve their outcome by changing their strategy alone.
- **Applicable**: Negotiation analysis, market competition
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Are you in a Nash Equilibrium? Can any party improve their position by changing strategy?"

### 67. BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement)
- **Core Principle**: Your negotiating power depends on your best alternative if the negotiation fails.
- **Applicable**: Any negotiation, salary discussion, vendor contract
- **Coaching Prompt**: "If this negotiation fails completely, what's your best alternative? How strong is it?"

### 68. Framing in Negotiation
- **Core Principle**: How an offer is presented (as a gain or loss) dramatically affects the other party's response.
- **Applicable**: Salary negotiation, sales, conflict resolution
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Are you framing this as what they'll gain or what they'll lose? Which frame is more persuasive?"

### 69. Tit for Tat
- **Core Principle**: In repeated interactions, start cooperative, then mirror the other party's behavior. Simple but highly effective.
- **Applicable**: Ongoing relationships, business partnerships
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Have you started with cooperation? Are you mirroring their behavior or escalating?"

### 70. Principled Negotiation
- **Core Principle**: Negotiate on interests, not positions. Separate people from the problem. Focus on options for mutual gain.
- **Applicable**: Difficult conversations, contract negotiations
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's your position? What's the underlying interest? Can we find a solution that serves both interests?"

### 71. Empathy Mapping
- **Core Principle**: Map what the other person thinks, feels, says, and does to build deeper understanding.
- **Applicable**: Conflict resolution, user research, communication
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Put yourself in their shoes. What are they thinking? Feeling? What do they fear?"

### 72. Active Listening
- **Core Principle**: Fully concentrate on what's being said rather than passively hearing or planning your response.
- **Applicable**: All conversations, conflict resolution, management
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Can you summarize what they just said in your own words? What did you hear beneath the words?"

### 73. Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Styles
- **Core Principle**: Five conflict styles: Competing, Collaborating, Compromising, Avoiding, Accommodating. Each appropriate in different contexts.
- **Applicable**: Conflict management, team dynamics
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's your default conflict style? Is it the right one for this situation?"

### 74. Win-Win Strategy
- **Core Principle**: Expand the pie before dividing it. Look for solutions where both parties gain.
- **Applicable**: Negotiation, partnership, team alignment
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What does a win look like for each side? Is there a way to make the pie bigger so both can win?"

## S08: Potential Release (10 Models)

### 75. Pygmalion Effect
- **Core Principle**: Higher expectations lead to improved performance. People tend to live up (or down) to what's expected of them.
- **Applicable**: Management, education, parenting
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What expectations are you communicating? Are they high enough? Have you explicitly stated them?"

### 76. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs
- **Core Principle**: People are motivated by unmet needs in a hierarchy: Physiological → Safety → Belonging → Esteem → Self-Actualization.
- **Applicable**: Team motivation, user needs analysis, marketing
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What level of needs is this person at? Are you addressing the right level?"

### 77. Social Proof
- **Core Principle**: People follow the actions of others, especially when uncertain. "Everyone else is doing it."
- **Applicable**: Marketing, behavior change, team culture
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Who are the role models? Can you make positive behaviors more visible to influence others?"

### 78. Reciprocity
- **Core Principle**: People feel obligated to return favors. Give first to create a sense of indebtedness.
- **Applicable**: Sales, relationship building, negotiation
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What can you give first? How can you create value before asking for anything?"

### 79. Scarcity Principle
- **Core Principle**: Things seem more valuable when they're less available. Limited time, limited quantity.
- **Applicable**: Marketing, urgency creation, motivation
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Is this genuinely scarce, or are you creating artificial scarcity? How can you use urgency ethically?"

### 80. Authority Principle
- **Core Principle**: People defer to experts and authority figures. Credibility and expertise are powerful persuasion tools.
- **Applicable**: Leadership, persuasion, presentations
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What establishes your authority on this topic? How can you credibly demonstrate expertise?"

### 81. Unity Principle
- **Core Principle**: People are more easily influenced by those with whom they share an identity or sense of belonging.
- **Applicable**: Team building, marketing, leadership
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What shared identity can you emphasize? How can you create a sense of 'we're in this together'?"

### 82. Two-Factor Theory
- **Core Principle**: Job satisfaction and dissatisfaction are driven by separate factors. Removing dissatisfaction doesn't create satisfaction.
- **Applicable**: Employee motivation, retention, management
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Are you addressing hygiene factors (salary, conditions) AND motivators (growth, meaning)?"

### 83. Situational Leadership
- **Core Principle**: Effective leadership adapts to the maturity level of the team: Directing → Coaching → Supporting → Delegating.
- **Applicable**: Management, team development
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's the maturity level of this person for this task? Which leadership style is appropriate?"

### 84. Psychological Safety
- **Core Principle**: Teams perform best when members feel safe to take risks, voice opinions, and make mistakes without fear of punishment.
- **Applicable**: Team building, innovation culture, management
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Do team members feel safe to speak up? What would make them feel safer?"

## S09: Market Strategy (10 Models)

### 85. Economic Moat
- **Core Principle**: A durable competitive advantage that protects a company's profits from competitors. Sources: Brand, Patents, Scale, Network Effects, Switching Costs.
- **Applicable**: Business strategy, investment analysis, competitive positioning
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What's your moat? Which of the 5 sources applies? How wide is it?"

### 86. Network Effects (strategic)
- **Core Principle**: Products become more valuable as more people use them. Creates powerful barriers to entry.
- **Applicable**: Platform businesses, market analysis
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Does your product have network effects? What's the critical mass needed?"

### 87. Switching Costs
- **Core Principle**: The cost (money, time, effort) for customers to switch from one product to another. Higher switching costs = more customer retention.
- **Applicable**: Product strategy, customer retention, pricing
- **Coaching Prompt**: "How easy is it for customers to leave? What can you do to increase switching costs ethically?"

### 88. Economies of Scale
- **Core Principle**: As production volume increases, the cost per unit decreases. Creates cost advantages over smaller competitors.
- **Applicable**: Business strategy, pricing, operations
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Where are your scale advantages? At what volume do costs significantly decrease?"

### 89. Brand Power
- **Core Principle**: A strong brand commands premium pricing, customer loyalty, and reduces customer acquisition costs.
- **Applicable**: Marketing, business valuation, competitive analysis
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What does your brand stand for? Would customers pay more for your brand vs. a generic alternative?"

### 90. Blue Ocean Strategy
- **Core Principle**: Instead of competing in bloody "red oceans" of existing markets, create uncontested "blue ocean" market space.
- **Applicable**: Innovation, market entry, strategy
- **Coaching Prompt**: "What are you competing on? Can you eliminate or reduce a factor the industry competes on, and create a new one?"

### 91. Value Chain Analysis
- **Core Principle**: Decompose a business into its primary and support activities to understand where value is created and costs accumulate.
- **Applicable**: Business strategy, cost optimization, competitive analysis
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Map your value chain. Where is the most value created? Where are the biggest costs?"

### 92. Porter's Five Forces
- **Core Principle**: Industry profitability is determined by five forces: Supplier power, Buyer power, Competitive rivalry, Threat of substitutes, Threat of new entrants.
- **Applicable**: Industry analysis, market entry, strategic planning
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Rate each of the five forces for your industry. Which is the strongest? How can you mitigate it?"

### 93. First-Mover Advantage
- **Core Principle**: Being first to market can provide advantages in brand recognition, customer loyalty, and switching costs — but isn't always decisive.
- **Applicable**: Market entry, product launches, innovation strategy
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Does first-mover advantage apply here? Or is this a market where fast-follower wins?"

### 94. Platform Strategy
- **Core Principle**: Create value by facilitating interactions between two or more distinct groups (e.g., buyers and sellers).
- **Applicable**: Digital businesses, marketplaces, ecosystem building
- **Coaching Prompt**: "Who are the sides of your platform? What's the chicken-and-egg problem? How do you solve it?"
